# Revision sheet — Chemistry for CSE Stream

Headings follow the CSE bank's grouping. Treat each as a prompt to reproduce a diagram and reaction.

## Module 1 — Electronic memory and liquid crystals

### Must know

- Memory is volatile or non-volatile; RAM is volatile, while ROM, EPROM, EEPROM and flash retain data. SRAM uses flip-flops; DRAM stores charge and needs refreshing.
- Thermotropic liquid crystals depend on temperature; lyotropic ones depend on concentration and temperature.
- Mesophases: nematic has no positional order; smectic forms layers; cholesteric is a twisted chiral nematic with a pitch.
- LCD uses a twisted nematic cell between polarisers; an applied field untwists the molecules and changes transmission.

### Definitions to reproduce

> A liquid crystal has properties between a conventional liquid and a crystalline solid; it has orientational order but little or no positional order.

## Module 2 — Electrochemistry and electrode systems

### Must know

- Nernst equation: E = E° + (0.0591/n) log [Mⁿ⁺] at 25 °C.
- A concentration cell has identical electrodes in the same electrolyte at different concentrations; E° = 0 and E = (0.0591/n) log(C₂/C₁).
- Calomel is a reference electrode: Hg/Hg₂Cl₂ in KCl. The glass electrode is ion-selective and its potential depends on pH.
- Conductometric titration follows conductance against titrant volume; potentiometric titration follows emf or ΔE/ΔV.

### Definitions to reproduce

> A concentration cell has two identical half-cells of the same composition that differ only in concentration.

## Module 3 — Polymers, fuel cells and solar cells

### Must know

- Addition polymerisation adds monomers; condensation eliminates water, HCl or NH₃. M̄ₙ = ΣnᵢMᵢ/Σnᵢ; M̄w = ΣnᵢMᵢ²/ΣnᵢMᵢ; PDI = M̄w/M̄ₙ ≥ 1.
- Conducting polymers need a conjugated π system. Doping creates polarons, bipolarons and solitons that carry charge.
- H₂–O₂ fuel cell: anode H₂ → 2H⁺ + 2e⁻; cathode ½O₂ + 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂O; overall H₂ + ½O₂ → H₂O.
- Solar cell: n-type and p-type silicon form a p–n junction; photons create electron–hole pairs and the junction separates them.

### Definitions to reproduce

> A conducting polymer is an organic polymer with a conjugated π-electron system that can carry current after doping.

## Module 4 — Corrosion and surface coatings

### Must know

- Electrochemical theory: anodic areas oxidise the metal, cathodic areas reduce O₂ or H⁺, and the circuit closes through the metal and electrolyte.
- Rusting of iron: Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻ at the anode; O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻ at the cathode; the product is hydrated ferric oxide.
- Differential aeration: the oxygen-poor region becomes anodic and corrodes. Waterline corrosion is differential aeration at the water line; pitting is localised breakdown of a passive film.
- Cathodic protection makes the metal the cathode: a sacrificial anode uses a more active metal; impressed current uses an external DC source.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Corrosion is the destruction of a metal by chemical or electrochemical reaction with its environment.

## Module 5 — Water quality and e-waste

### Must know

- BOD is the dissolved oxygen used by aerobic organisms at 20 °C over five days. COD is the oxygen needed to oxidise organic and inorganic matter chemically. COD is higher and faster.
- Sewage treatment: primary removes solids, secondary uses activated sludge, tertiary removes nutrients and dissolved salts.
- Hardness is caused by Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺. Temporary hardness is due to bicarbonates and is removed by boiling; permanent hardness is due to chlorides and sulphates.
- Ion exchange: cation resin replaces Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ with H⁺; anion resin replaces anions with OH⁻; regenerated with HCl and NaOH.
- Gold is recovered from e-waste by cyanide leaching and zinc precipitation, or by aqua regia.

### Definitions to reproduce

> BOD is the amount of dissolved oxygen required by aerobic micro-organisms to oxidise organic matter at 20 °C over five days.
