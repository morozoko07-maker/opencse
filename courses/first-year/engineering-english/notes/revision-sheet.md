## Exam pattern

The SEE is 50 marks in one hour: fifty multiple-choice questions of one mark each, with no negative marking. CIE is 50, from two 30-mark tests and two 20-mark assignments, scaled down.

## Module 1 — Introduction to communicative English

### Must know

- Communication is the exchange of ideas, information or feelings between a sender and a receiver.
- The process: sender, encoding, message, channel, receiver, decoding, feedback and noise.
- Barriers may be physical, semantic, psychological, cultural or organisational.
- Feedback confirms that the message was received and understood; without it, communication is incomplete.
- Verbal communication uses words; non-verbal communication uses body language, gesture, posture, dress and tone.
- Intrapersonal communication is within oneself; interpersonal communication is between two or more people.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Communication is the process of exchanging ideas, thoughts and feelings between a sender and a receiver through a chosen channel.

> A barrier is any factor that interferes with the transmission or understanding of a message.

## Module 2 — Introduction to phonetics

### Must know

- English has 44 sounds: 20 vowel sounds (12 pure vowels and 8 diphthongs) and 24 consonant sounds.
- Phonetics is the study of speech sounds; the IPA gives one symbol per sound.
- A diphthong is a glide from one vowel to another within one syllable.
- A syllable is a unit of pronunciation with one vowel sound; division affects spelling and stress.
- Some letters are silent, as in `fasten`, `wreath` and `handkerchief`.
- Word accent is the stressed syllable in a word; sentence stress falls on important words.
- Mother-tongue influence affects pronunciation; neutralisation needs listening, drill and contrastive practice.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Phonetics is the branch of linguistics that studies the production, transmission and perception of speech sounds.

> A diphthong is a single syllable containing a glide from one vowel sound to another.

## Module 3 — Introduction to English grammar

### Must know

- The eight parts of speech: noun, pronoun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition, conjunction and interjection.
- `A` and `an` are indefinite articles; `the` is definite. Use `an` before a vowel sound, not merely a vowel letter.
- Prepositions show relations of place, time, direction and manner.
- Word formation uses prefixes and suffixes: `il-` in `illiterate`, `un-` in `unhappy`, `-ness` in `happiness`.
- One-word substitution replaces a phrase with a single word.
- A question tag repeats the auxiliary and reverses polarity: a positive statement takes a negative tag.
- Strong and weak forms change vowel quality in connected speech.

### Definitions to reproduce

> A question tag is a short question added to a statement to ask for confirmation or agreement.

> An affix is a morpheme attached to a root word, either as a prefix or as a suffix.

## Module 4 — Grammar and vocabulary

### Must know

- Tense combines time (present, past, future) with aspect (simple, continuous, perfect, perfect continuous).
- The sequence of tenses keeps the tense of the reported clause consistent with the reporting verb.
- Abbreviations shorten written forms; contractions combine words with an apostrophe.
- Minimal pairs differ in one sound, as in `ship` and `sheep`.
- `Since` and `for` are used with perfect tenses; `yesterday` takes the simple past.

### Definitions to reproduce

> A contraction is a shortened form of a word or phrase in which an apostrophe marks the omitted letters.

> A minimal pair is a pair of words that differ in only one sound but have different meanings.

## Module 5 — Communication skills for employment

### Must know

- Information transfer converts prose into tables, charts, diagrams or notes, or the reverse.
- An oral presentation needs a clear structure: introduction, body, conclusion, and visual aids.
- Extempore speaking is unprepared; public speaking is usually planned and rehearsed.
- Good delivery uses eye contact, audible pace, pauses and controlled gestures.
- Reading comprehension needs skimming for gist and scanning for detail.
- Listening comprehension needs attention to main ideas, specific details and the speaker's tone.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Information transfer is the presentation of the same information in a different form, such as a passage as a table or a table as a paragraph.

> Extempore speaking is speaking on a topic with little or no preparation.
