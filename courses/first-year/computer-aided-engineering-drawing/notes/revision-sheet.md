# Revision sheet — Computer-Aided Engineering Drawing

Drawing is marked on construction. Work each problem on a fresh sheet with faint projection lines. The bank is practice problems, no marks.

## Drawing fundamentals and CAD setup

### Must know

- First-angle projection is the BIS standard: the object sits between the observer and the plane, so the top view is below the front view.
- HP carries the top view, VP the front view, RPP and LPP the side views. Points are labelled a, a′ and a″.
- Continuous thick lines show visible outlines, dashed lines hidden edges, chain lines centre lines, thin lines dimensions and construction.
- Dimensioning gives true sizes; angular dimensions are in degrees and are never scaled.
- CAD packages include AutoCAD, CATIA, SolidWorks and Pro/E.

### Definitions to reproduce

> The horizontal plane (HP) is the reference plane on which the top view is drawn.

## Module 2 — Projection of straight lines

### Must know

- A line has a true length and true inclinations; its projections are shorter and show apparent lengths and apparent inclinations.
- The distance between end projectors, measured parallel to XY, fixes the relative positions of the ends.
- Rotating-line method: draw the line parallel to one plane for true length, then rotate it to the required inclination.
- Auxiliary-plane method: use a plane parallel to the line to obtain true length and inclination directly.

### Definitions to reproduce

> True length is the actual length of a line; apparent length is the length of its projection on a reference plane.

## Module 3 — Projection of plane surfaces

### Must know

- A lamina is a thin plane figure. Its surface may be parallel to a plane, inclined to one plane, or inclined to both.
- When a lamina rests on an edge on HP, that edge is in HP and the surface is inclined to HP.
- Start with the lamina parallel to the plane where its true shape is seen, then tilt it to the required angle.
- A surface inclined to HP but perpendicular to VP appears as an edge in the front view.

### Definitions to reproduce

> A plane surface has length and breadth but negligible thickness.

## Projection of solids

### Must know

- A solid has three dimensions. Polyhedra have plane faces; solids of revolution have curved surfaces.
- Prisms and cylinders have two parallel bases; pyramids and cones have one base and an apex.
- A solid may rest on its base, a corner, an edge or a generator; each position changes the first view.
- Change-of-position method: draw the simple position, tilt the axis, then rotate the plan to the required angle.
- Project all base corners, then the apex or top face, then join visible edges; hidden edges are dashed.

### Definitions to reproduce

> A solid is a three-dimensional object having length, breadth and thickness.

## Development of lateral surfaces

### Must know

- Development is the true shape of a surface unfolded onto a plane.
- Parallel-line method for prisms and cylinders: divide the base perimeter into equal parts and set them along a straight line at true height.
- Radial-line method for pyramids and cones: use the true length of the slant edge or generator as radius.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Development is the true shape of a surface obtained by unfolding it onto a plane.

## Last 24 hours

1. Draw one line inclined to both HP and VP and mark both apparent lengths.
2. Draw one triangular lamina resting on an edge and inclined to both planes.
3. Draw one prism resting on a corner and one pyramid resting on an edge.
4. Develop one truncated prism.

## Question bank

**Projection of straight lines**

1. A line AB 80 mm long has end A 20 mm above HP and 30 mm in front of VP. It is inclined at 30° to HP and 45° to VP. Draw the projections and find the apparent lengths and apparent inclinations.

**Projection of plane surfaces**

2. An equilateral triangular lamina of 25 mm side lies with one edge on HP, the surface inclined at 60° to HP, and that edge inclined at 60° to VP. Draw the projections.
3. A square lamina of 40 mm side rests on one side on HP. The lamina makes 30° to HP and the resting side makes 45° to VP. Draw the projections.

**Projection of solids**

4. A square prism with 35 mm base sides and 60 mm axis rests on HP on one corner of its base, with the two edges at that corner equally inclined to HP. Draw the projections when the axis is inclined at 40° to HP and appears inclined at 45° to VP.

**Development of lateral surfaces**

5. A square prism of base side 30 mm and axis 60 mm rests on HP with all vertical faces equally inclined to VP. It is cut by a plane 60° to HP, perpendicular to VP, passing through a point on the axis 15 mm from the top. Develop the lower portion.
