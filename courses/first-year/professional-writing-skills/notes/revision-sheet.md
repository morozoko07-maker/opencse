## Module 1 — Grammar and common errors

### Must know

- Parts of speech: noun, pronoun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition, conjunction and interjection.
- A verb must agree with its subject in number and person. Two singular nouns joined by "and" take a plural verb unless they form one idea: "bread and butter is".
- With "either … or" and "neither … nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject.
- Collective nouns take a singular verb when acting as one unit, and a plural verb when the members act separately.
- Auxiliary verbs (be, have, do, shall, will, can, may, must) carry tense, voice, mood or negation. Phrasal verbs combine a verb with a particle: "give up", "put off".
- Confused pairs: advice/advise, affect/effect, complement/compliment, principal/principle, flaunt/flout.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Subject–verb agreement is the rule that a verb must match its subject in number and person.

> A phrasal verb is a verb combined with an adverb or preposition that together carry a new meaning.

## Module 2 — Paragraphs and precis

### Must know

- A paragraph has a topic sentence, supporting sentences and a concluding sentence. The topic sentence states the main idea and gives the paragraph direction.
- Supporting sentences explain, define, illustrate or give evidence. The conclusion restates the main point and adds no new idea.
- Unity means every sentence serves the main idea; coherence means the sentences are linked in a clear order.
- A precis is a shortened version of a passage in the writer's own words. It should be about one-third of the original, with a title and all essential points.
- A precis must not add opinion, criticism, examples or abbreviations, and must not change the passage's meaning.

### Definitions to reproduce

> A paragraph is a group of sentences that develop one central idea.

> A precis is a concise, accurate summary of a passage written in the writer's own words.

## Business correspondence — email and memorandum

### Must know

- A formal email has a clear subject, a greeting, an opening that states the purpose, a short body, a courteous close and a signature block.
- Use "Dear Sir/Madam" with "Yours faithfully"; use "Dear Mr/Mrs/Ms Surname" with "Yours sincerely".
- Cc sends a copy to another recipient; Bcc sends a blind copy without disclosing that address.
- A memorandum is an internal document. It has a header (To, From, Date, Subject), an introduction, a statement of facts, an argument and a conclusion.

### Definitions to reproduce

> A memorandum is a short internal message that informs, requests action or recommends a course of action.

> Cc is a copy sent to a recipient other than the main addressee; Bcc is a copy sent without revealing that address to the others.

## Employment communication — CV, resume, bio-data and interviews

### Must know

- A CV is a full record of education, skills, jobs and affiliations, usually two to three pages and in chronological order. It suits fresh graduates and academic applications.
- A resume is a short, targeted summary, usually one or two pages, written in bullet points and customised to the job.
- Bio-data emphasises personal particulars such as date of birth, nationality, marital status and religion. It is common in India for government applications.
- An interview tests subject knowledge, communication and fit. Prepare examples and listen to the full question before answering.
- In listening, distinguish appreciative, critical, relationship (empathetic) and discriminative listening. Barriers include interrupting, faking attention, prejudice, noise and excessive talking.

### Definitions to reproduce

> A curriculum vitae is a detailed account of a person's education, qualifications and work history.

> A resume is a brief, job-specific summary of skills and experience.

## Reading skills

### Must know

- Skimming reads rapidly for the main idea or gist.
- Scanning reads rapidly for one specific fact, such as a date or name.
- Extensive reading covers a longer text for general understanding; intensive reading covers a short text closely for detail.
- Separate fact from opinion and identify the writer's argument and counter-argument.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Skimming is rapid reading for the main points; scanning is rapid reading for a specific piece of information.
