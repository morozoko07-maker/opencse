# Revision sheet — Indian Constitution

The paper is multiple choice, so exact names, articles, parts and dates matter. Learn the numbers as a table and revise each office separately.

## Exam pattern

Both model papers give 1 hour and 50 marks: 50 compulsory MCQs, one mark each, with no negative marking. Shading more than one option rejects that question.

## Module 1 — Constitution: meaning, making and features

### Must know

- The Constituent Assembly had Dr Rajendra Prasad as chairman and Dr B. R. Ambedkar as chairman of the drafting committee.
- The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 and came into force on 26 January 1950. Framing took 2 years, 11 months and 18 days.
- The Government of India Act 1935 was the most profound single influence.
- In the form adopted it had 395 Articles and 8 Schedules and is the longest written constitution.

### Definitions to reproduce

> A constitution is a set of basic laws or principles that describe the rights and duties of citizens and the way the state is governed.

> A republic is a state in which the head of state is elected and there is no monarchy.

## Module 2 — Preamble and Fundamental Rights

### Must know

- The Preamble secures justice (social, economic, political), liberty, equality and fraternity.
- Fundamental Rights are in Part III and are justiciable. A law that contravenes them is void.
- The six groups are equality, freedom, against exploitation, freedom of religion, cultural and educational rights, and constitutional remedies.
- The five writs are habeas corpus, mandamus, certiorari, prohibition and quo warranto.
- Rights carry reasonable restrictions in the public interest. The right to property was removed from Fundamental Rights by the 44th Amendment.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Fundamental Rights are the basic rights guaranteed by Part III and enforceable in the courts.

## Module 3 — Directive Principles and Fundamental Duties

### Must know

- Directive Principles are in Part IV. They are not enforceable in court but guide the state towards a welfare state.
- Examples: equal pay, panchayats, a uniform civil code, separation of the judiciary, and international peace.
- Fundamental Duties are in Part IVA and were added by the 42nd Amendment. The 86th Amendment added the parent's duty to educate a child.
- Fundamental Rights are enforceable; Directive Principles and Fundamental Duties are not directly enforceable.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Directive Principles of State Policy are guidelines in Part IV that direct the state in making policy, though they are not enforceable in court.

## Module 4 — Union and state government

### Must know

- The President holds office for five years, is elected by an electoral college, must be at least 35, and is impeached under Article 61.
- The President is the nominal executive; the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers form the real executive.
- Parliament has the President, Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha. A Money Bill can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha.
- Supreme Court judges retire at 65; High Court judges at 62. The Chief Justice of India is appointed by the President.
- The Governor is appointed by the President, holds office during the President's pleasure, and normally serves five years. The Chief Minister is appointed by the Governor.

### Definitions to reproduce

> The President is the constitutional head of the Union and exercises executive power on the advice of the Council of Ministers.

## Module 5 — Elections, emergency and amendments

### Must know

- The Election Commission is set up under Article 324 and consists of the Chief Election Commissioner and other Election Commissioners.
- National emergency is under Article 352; state emergency under Article 356; financial emergency under Article 360.
- The 42nd Amendment is the lengthiest amendment. The 44th replaced "internal disturbance" with "armed rebellion" for national emergency.
- The right to education was introduced by the 86th Amendment, which added Article 21A.

### Definitions to reproduce

> A financial emergency is declared under Article 360 when the President is satisfied that the financial stability of India is threatened.

## Last 24 hours

1. Memorise the Constituent Assembly names, the two dates and the 395/8 figures.
2. Make a table of Part III, Part IV and Part IVA: content and enforceability.
3. Learn the tenure figures for President, Governor, Supreme Court and High Court.
