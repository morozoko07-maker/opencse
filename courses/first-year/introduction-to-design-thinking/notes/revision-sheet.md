# Revision sheet — Introduction to Design Thinking

The syllabus gives five modules; the MCQ banks number their sets differently, so the questions below are grouped by topic. Every bank question carries one mark; there is no descriptive paper.

## Exam pattern

The SEE is 50 marks in one hour: fifty multiple-choice questions of one mark each. CIE is 50, from two 30-mark tests, two 10-mark assignments and a 20-mark course project, scaled down.

## Module 1 — Introduction to design thinking

### Must know

- Design thinking is a human-centred, iterative approach to framing and solving problems.
- The three I's are Inspiration, Ideation and Implementation.
- The five stages are Empathise, Define, Ideate, Prototype and Test; they are not linear.
- The double diamond has four phases: Discover, Define, Develop and Deliver, alternating divergent and convergent thinking.
- An MVP is the smallest version of a product that lets users test the core value.
- PESTEL scans Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental and Legal factors.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Design thinking is a human-centred process for defining, framing and solving problems from the user's perspective.

> A minimum viable product is the smallest working version of a product that allows useful feedback from users.

## Module 2 — Observation and empathetic design

### Must know

- Empathetic design observes customers in their own setting, not in an artificial laboratory.
- Shadowing is observing a user by taking part in the situation.
- AEIOU records Activities, Environments, Interactions, Objects and Users.
- An empathy map records what a user says, thinks, does and feels.
- A customer journey maps the before, during and after of a service.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Empathetic design is the observation of users in their real environment so that the designer can understand their situation and needs.

> An empathy map is a visual record of a user's words, thoughts, actions and feelings.

## Module 3 — Define, ideate and strategic innovation

### Must know

- The point-of-view phase states the user, the need and the insight.
- A persona is an evidence-based profile of a target user.
- Jobs-to-be-done describes what a user is trying to accomplish, not just what they buy.
- A good How-Might-We question is narrow enough to act on and broad enough to allow ideas.
- Brainstorming rules: defer judgement, encourage wild ideas, build on others and stay on topic.
- Mind mapping starts from a central idea and radiates branches, arrows and sub-topics.
- Strategic innovation links growth, predictability, foresight, change and value redefinition.

### Definitions to reproduce

> A persona is a fictional profile that represents a group of target users and their goals.

> A How-Might-We question reframes a problem statement as an actionable design challenge.

## Module 4 — Evaluating ideas and innovation culture

### Must know

- Assessment areas include desirability, feasibility and viability, often shortened to DFV.
- The PPCO method lists Pluses, Potentials, Concerns and Overcomes.
- SWOT records Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities and Threats.
- TRIZ is the theory of inventive problem solving, built on patterns of invention and contradiction.
- An innovation culture needs psychological safety, time, autonomy and tolerance of failure.

### Definitions to reproduce

> A SWOT analysis identifies the strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats of an idea or organisation.

> TRIZ is a systematic method for solving inventive problems by resolving technical contradictions.

## Module 5 — Prototype and testing

### Must know

- A prototype is a scaled-down, quickly assembled, low-cost model for testing, not a final product.
- Storyboarding sketches the user's sequence of actions; storytelling frames the problem for an audience.
- Test with real users, observe what they do and ask open questions.
- Interviews encourage long answers; surveys suit closed questions at scale.
- Agile design works in short iterations with frequent feedback.
- Scrum has three roles (Product Owner, Scrum Master, development team) and three artefacts (product backlog, sprint backlog, increment).

### Definitions to reproduce

> A prototype is an early model of a product, built to test a concept or process with users.

> Scrum is an agile framework in which a small team delivers working increments in fixed short cycles called sprints.

## Last 24 hours

1. Write the five stages and one activity for each.
2. Draw the double diamond and label its four phases.
3. Prepare one empathy map and one persona.
4. Write five HMW questions for a campus problem.
