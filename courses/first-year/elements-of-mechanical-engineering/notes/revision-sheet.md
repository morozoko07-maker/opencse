# Revision sheet — Elements of Mechanical Engineering

The folder has a handwritten list for Modules 1–2 and typed banks for Modules 3–5. No marks are printed.

## Exam pattern

The SEE is 100 marks in three hours, scaled to 50: five modules, two 20-mark questions per module, one from each. CIE is 50.

## Module 1 — Energy sources, refrigeration and air conditioning

### Must know

- Fossil fuels are solid (wood, peat, lignite, coal), liquid (petrol, kerosene, diesel) and gaseous (natural gas, coal gas, producer gas).
- Solar conversion routes: heliochemical (photosynthesis), helioelectrical (photovoltaic) and heliothermal (heat). A flat-plate collector absorbs radiation on a blackened plate and transfers heat to a fluid.
- A windmill converts wind energy into rotary motion; a hydro station converts stored-water energy into electricity through a turbine.
- Vapour-compression refrigeration has four parts: compressor, condenser, expansion valve and evaporator.
- COP = refrigerating effect / work input; one ton of refrigeration is 3.5 kW.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Refrigeration is the process of removing heat from a space to keep it below the surrounding temperature.

> The coefficient of performance is the ratio of heat removed in the evaporator to the work supplied to the compressor.

## Module 2 — IC engines, electric vehicles and hybrids

### Must know

- An IC engine burns fuel inside the cylinder and converts the heat into mechanical work.
- Four-stroke petrol engine: suction, compression, power and exhaust, with one power stroke in two revolutions.
- A petrol engine uses a spark plug; a diesel engine uses high compression and fuel injection, giving higher efficiency.
- Engine terms: TDC, BDC, bore, stroke, swept volume, clearance volume and compression ratio.
- An EV has a battery, motor, controller, charger and drivetrain. A hybrid uses an engine and a motor; in a series hybrid the engine drives a generator, and in a parallel hybrid both drive the wheels.

### Definitions to reproduce

> An internal combustion engine is a heat engine in which fuel burns inside the cylinder to produce mechanical work.

## Module 3 — Metal joining and power transmission

### Must know

- Joining processes are permanent (welding, soldering, brazing) or temporary (bolts, rivets, keys).
- Soldering uses a filler that melts below the base-metal melting point; flux removes oxide.
- Arc welding uses an electric arc to melt the joint. TIG uses a non-consumable tungsten electrode and separate filler; MIG uses a continuous consumable wire and shielding gas.
- Gear drives give a positive drive and a constant velocity ratio in less space, but need lubrication and accurate alignment.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Welding is a permanent joining process that coalesces materials by heat, pressure or both.

## Module 4 — CNC machines and automation

### Must know

- CNC means computer numerical control: a computer reads a part program and controls the machine axes.
- Elements: part program, machine control unit, drives, feedback devices and the machine tool. Open-loop has no feedback; closed-loop uses it.
- Automation types: fixed (one product, high volume), programmable (batch work) and flexible (several products, quick changeover).

### Definitions to reproduce

> Automation is the use of control systems to operate equipment with little or no human intervention.

## Module 5 — Robotics and IoT

### Must know

- A robot has a manipulator, actuators, sensors, a controller and a power supply.
- Joints are revolute or prismatic; configurations are polar, cylindrical, cartesian and articulated.
- Applications: material handling, welding, painting, assembly, inspection and pick-and-place.
- IoT functional blocks: sensing, processing, connectivity, power and application. Communication models: request-response, publish-subscribe, push-pull and exclusive pair.

### Definitions to reproduce

> A robot is a programmable multifunction manipulator designed to move material, parts, tools or devices through programmed motions.

## Last 24 hours

1. Draw the vapour-compression cycle and label its four parts.
2. Draw the four strokes of a petrol engine and the P-V diagram.
3. Derive the length of an open belt drive.
4. List the CNC elements and the automation types.

## Question bank

1. Discuss briefly various types of gaseous fuels (at least five).
2. Explain various types of solid fuels (at least five).
3. Describe various types of liquid fuels (at least five).
4. Explain the working of a solar flat-plate collector with a neat sketch, and state its merits and demerits.
5. Explain the working of a hydro power station with a neat sketch.
6. Define and describe: ice-making capacity, COP, tons of refrigeration, specific humidity, relative humidity and air conditioning.
7. Explain the working of a vapour-compression refrigeration system with a neat sketch.
8. Give the broad classification of IC engines, and compare a petrol engine with a diesel engine.
9. Draw the P-V diagram for petrol and diesel engines and explain all processes.
10. Explain the principle of TIG welding with a neat labelled diagram.
11. Derive the length of an open belt drive with a diagram.
12. State the advantages and disadvantages of gear drives over belt drives.
13. Define CNC and explain the basic components of a CNC system.
14. Define robot and give its advantages and disadvantages.
15. Describe the functional blocks of an IoT system and list its communication models.
