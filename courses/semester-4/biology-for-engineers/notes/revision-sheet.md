## Module 1 — Cell biology and biomolecules

### Must know

- The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life.
- A plant cell has a cellulose wall, chloroplasts and a large vacuole; an animal cell has centrioles and lysosomes.
- Carbohydrates are (CH₂O)ₙ; sugars are sweet and soluble, polysaccharides store or support.
- Proteins are amino-acid chains with four structure levels.
- Lipids are hydrophobic; they store energy, build membranes, insulate and act as hormone precursors.
- Nucleic acids store genetic information and direct protein synthesis.
- Enzymes are biological catalysts with an active site, specificity and a denaturation point.
- Stem cells self-renew and can differentiate; potency ranges from totipotent to unipotent.

### Definitions to reproduce

> A cell is the smallest structural and functional unit of life, bounded by a membrane and containing hereditary material.

> An enzyme speeds up a biochemical reaction by lowering its activation energy without being consumed.

## Module 2 — Biomolecules in engineering

### Must know

- Protein properties depend on amino-acid sequence, charge, solubility and folding; heat or pH denatures them.
- Whey protein is a complete protein used in supplements and food formulations.
- DNA fingerprinting amplifies STRs by PCR, runs gel electrophoresis and compares bands.
- Cellulose-based water filters work by adsorption, mechanical trapping and chemical or biological filtration.
- PHAs and PLA are biodegradable microbial or plant-derived polyesters used as plastics.
- An enzyme biosensor couples a specific enzyme reaction to a transducer that produces an electrical signal.
- RNA vaccines deliver mRNA in lipid nanoparticles so cells make an antigen; DNA vaccines deliver DNA to the nucleus.
- Meat analogues use plant proteins or fungal protein to mimic the texture of meat.

### Definitions to reproduce

> DNA fingerprinting identifies an individual from the pattern of repeated DNA sequences.

> A biosensor couples a biological recognition element to a transducer that produces a measurable signal.

## Module 3 — The human body as engineering systems

### Must know

- The heart is a four-chambered pump with valves and two circuits.
- The eye focuses light through the cornea and lens onto the retina, which acts as the sensor.
- The kidney filters blood in the nephron by glomerular filtration, then reabsorbs useful solutes and water.
- The brain processes information through neurons and synapses and stores it by changing connection strengths.
- Myopia is corrected with a concave lens; hypermetropia with a convex lens.
- A stent holds an artery open; a pacemaker supplies electrical impulses when the natural rhythm fails.
- Dialysis replaces kidney filtration by diffusion and ultrafiltration across a membrane.
- A CPU and a brain both process signals, but the brain is parallel, plastic and far more energy-efficient.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Homeostasis maintains a stable internal environment within narrow limits.

> A pacemaker delivers electrical impulses when the heart's natural rhythm is too slow or irregular.

## Module 4 — Bio-inspired design

### Must know

- Echolocation emits a pulse and measures the returning echo's delay and frequency change.
- Bats and dolphins use biosonar; technical sonar and ultrasound use the same pulse–echo principle.
- The kingfisher's streamlined beak inspired the bullet train nose, reducing the tunnel boom.
- Bird flight is compared with GPS: satellites, a control segment and a receiver compute position.
- Shark denticles are riblet-like scales that reduce drag and turbulence.
- The lotus leaf is superhydrophobic because of micro- and nano-scale wax structures; water rolls off and cleans it.
- A bionic leaf uses a catalyst to split water and reduce carbon dioxide, mimicking photosynthesis.
- Blood substitutes include perfluorocarbons and haemoglobin-based carriers, with oxygen-carrying and safety limits.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Biomimetics designs materials, structures or systems from biological functions.

> Echolocation locates objects by emitting sound and interpreting the echoes.

## Module 5 — Bioengineering applications

### Must know

- An electronic tongue uses taste sensors to classify liquids for food quality.
- An electronic nose uses gas sensors to fingerprint aromas and detect spoilage.
- Bioprinting deposits bioink and living cells layer by layer to build tissue constructs.
- A 3D printer uses plastic or metal; a bioprinter uses bioink and cells.
- Scaffolds give cells a porous, mechanically stable support; the skeletal and muscular systems are natural examples.
- Bio-concrete uses bacteria to precipitate calcite and seal cracks, improving durability.
- AI diagnosis learns patterns from images and records, but needs clean data and clinical validation.
- Microbial adsorption binds heavy metals such as mercury and arsenic to cell surfaces.

### Definitions to reproduce

> A scaffold is a porous temporary support that gives cells shape and mechanical cues.

> Bioremediation uses living organisms or their products to remove contaminants.
