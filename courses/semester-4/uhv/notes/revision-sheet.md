# Revision sheet — Universal Human Values

The banks are multiple-choice, so the question bank below keeps the stems and drops the options. The module order follows the bank files; the file named `Module4_MCQ.pdf` is headed "Module 3 — Harmony in Nature" internally, and is treated here as the nature module.

## Exam pattern

The README gives CIE 50 + SEE 50. No SEE paper is present. The Module 3 and 4 banks print one mark per MCQ; the others print none.

## Module 1 — Value education and self-exploration

### Must know

- Values are our participation in the larger order, and are an outcome of realisation and understanding.
- Value education is universal, natural and verifiable, all-encompassing, rational and leads to harmony.
- Basic human aspirations are continuous happiness and prosperity.
- The four levels of living are self, family, society, and nature or existence.
- Self-exploration is a dialogue between what I am and what I really want to be.
- Its two mechanisms are natural acceptance and experiential validation.
- Natural acceptance is innate and unconditional; preconditioning is an assumed belief.
- Right understanding is the basis of ethical human conduct.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Value education is education that develops the clarity and commitment to live in harmony at all levels of living.

> Self-exploration is the process of examining one's own desires, thoughts and assumptions against natural acceptance.

## Module 2 — Harmony in the self and body

### Must know

- A human being is the co-existence of the self (I) and the body.
- The self is consciousness; the body is material.
- The self needs happiness, trust and respect, which are continuous.
- The body needs food, water, air and physical facility, which are temporary.
- Activities of the self are knowing, assuming, recognising and fulfilling.
- Happiness is harmony, and prosperity is the feeling of having more than required physical facilities.
- The body needs right utilisation, protection and nurturing.
- Self-regulation, or sanyam, keeps the body and senses in order.

### Definitions to reproduce

> The self is the conscious part of a human being that desires, thinks, recognises and feels.

> Happiness is the state of being in harmony, which I like to continue.

## Module 3 — Harmony in the family and society

### Must know

- The family is the basic unit of human society and the first place where relationship is learnt.
- Relationship is between one self and another self, not between bodies.
- Feelings in relationship are definite and can be recognised and fulfilled.
- The nine feelings are trust, respect, affection, care, guidance, reverence, glory, gratitude and love.
- Trust is the foundation value; respect is right evaluation of the other.
- The five systems are education, health, production, justice, and exchange–storage.
- The human goal is right understanding, prosperity, fearlessness and co-existence.
- Justice recognises relationship and ensures mutual fulfilment.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Trust is the feeling that the other intends to make me happy and has the competence to do so.

> Respect is the right evaluation of the other's self, not an evaluation of the body or possessions.

## Module 4 — Harmony in nature and existence

### Must know

- Nature has four orders: physical, bio, animal and human.
- Units in nature are self-organised and coexist with all-pervading space.
- The first three orders live in mutual fulfilment; the human order must enrich all four.
- Co-existence is the underlying reality; self-regulation and mutual fulfilment express it.
- The physical order includes air, water and metals; the bio order includes plants and trees.
- Human interference breaks natural cycles and causes pollution and climate change.
- Sustainability follows from understanding the order and recyclability of nature.
- Harmony at this level means human beings are fulfilling to the other orders.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Co-existence is the reality that every unit lives in relation with every other unit and with space.

> The four orders of nature are the physical, bio, animal and human orders.

## Module 5 — Professional ethics and universal human order

### Must know

- Professional ethics is right understanding applied in one's profession.
- Values in profession include honesty, integrity, responsibility, competence and respect.
- Competence without character is incomplete; both are needed.
- Ethical conduct is naturally acceptable, universal and verifiable.
- A profession is a means of participation in society, not only a source of income.
- Unethical practice grows from a lack of right understanding and misplaced aspirations.
- A moral decision considers motive, means and consequences.
- The universal human order has mutually enriching institutions and people.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Professional ethics is the application of right understanding and human values in professional life.

> The universal human order is a social order in which every person and institution participates in mutual enrichment.

## Last 24 hours

1. Recite the five guidelines of value education and the two mechanisms of self-exploration.
2. Draw the self–body distinction as a table of needs and activities.
3. List the nine feelings in relationship and define trust and respect.
4. Name the five social systems and the four components of the human goal.
5. State the four orders of nature and one example of mutual fulfilment.
6. Define professional ethics and list five professional values.
7. Recall the difference between natural acceptance and preconditioning.
