# Revision sheet — Automotive Technology

Automotive Technology (BME704D) is a three-credit open elective. The handout divides it into five modules: engine components and cooling; transmission and brakes; steering, suspension and ignition; supercharging and fuel systems; and emissions and electric vehicles.

## Exam pattern

The handout gives CIE 50 and SEE 50, with a three-hour descriptive SEE. Internal-assessment and question-bank items are mostly 10 marks, so combine a labelled sketch with four or five explanatory points.

## Module 1 — Engine components, cooling and lubrication

### Must know

- SI versus CI: a spark plug starts combustion in SI; compressed-air temperature starts it in CI.
- Cylinder arrangements: inline, opposed, V and radial, with balance and compactness merits.
- Dry and wet liners, piston and rings, connecting rod, crankshaft, valves and valve actuation.
- Valve and port timing; SI and CI combustion chambers; swirl by induction, compression and combustion-induced methods.
- Thermosiphon and pump cooling; radiator, water pump and wax thermostat.
- Splash and pressure lubrication; hydrodynamic and boundary lubrication; HCCI, hybrid and electric cars.

### Definitions to reproduce

**TDC** is the piston's highest position. **Compression ratio** is total cylinder volume divided by clearance volume. A **dry liner** is supported along its full length by the block; a **wet liner** touches coolant directly and is sealed at top and bottom.

### Worked pattern

For dry versus wet liners, draw two sections and use five rows: coolant contact, leak-proof joint, block construction, cooling and machining. Dry: no contact, no joint, complex block, poorer cooling. Wet: direct contact, joint needed, simpler block, better cooling.

## Module 2 — Transmission and brakes

### Must know

- Clutch types; manual and automatic gearboxes; synchromesh and double declutching.
- Overdrive, fluid flywheel, torque converter, propeller shaft, slip and universal joints.
- Differential and rear axle; Hotchkiss and torque tube drives.
- Mechanical, hydraulic, vacuum and air brakes; master and wheel cylinders; drum and disc brakes.
- ABS: slip ratio, wheel-speed sensors, hydraulic control unit, and increase, hold and decrease modes.
- Weight transfer and the effect of friction on stopping distance.

### Definitions to reproduce

A **clutch** connects and disconnects engine and transmission. A **torque converter** is a fluid coupling with a stator that multiplies torque at large speed differences. **Hotchkiss drive** uses leaf springs for torque reaction and driving thrust; **torque tube drive** encloses the propeller shaft in a tube that takes those loads, so one universal joint suffices.

### Worked pattern

For Hotchkiss versus torque tube, tabulate the members carrying torque reaction, number of universal joints, need for a sliding joint, and axle movement. Sketch Hotchkiss with two universal joints and a sliding joint, and torque tube with a spherical cup at the frame.

## Module 3 — Steering, suspension and ignition

### Must know

- Steering geometry: camber, caster, toe-in, toe-out, kingpin inclination, included angle and scrub radius.
- Worm-and-wheel, worm-and-nut and recirculating-ball steering gears; power steering.
- Leaf, coil and torsion-bar springs; independent and air suspension.
- Battery, magneto and electronic ignition; firing order and advance.

### Definitions to reproduce

**Camber** is wheel tilt from vertical. **Caster** is tilt of the kingpin axis in the wheel plane; positive caster gives directional stability. **Toe-in** means wheel fronts are closer than their backs. **Kingpin inclination** is inward tilt of the steering axis. **Scrub radius** is the distance between steering-axis and tyre-centre intersections at the road.

## Module 4 — Superchargers, fuels and injection

### Must know

- Forced induction, boost and volumetric efficiency.
- Centrifugal, Roots and vane superchargers; turbocharger, wastegate, intercooler and turbo lag.
- Conventional and alternative fuels; normal and abnormal combustion; octane and cetane numbers.
- SI mixture needs at idle, cruising and high power; carburettor principle and the Solex carburettor.
- Single-point, multi-point and sequential injection; pumps, filters, injectors and common-rail diesel injection.

### Definitions to reproduce

**Supercharging** raises intake density above atmospheric pressure. **Turbo lag** is the delay while exhaust energy spins the turbine to useful boost. **Octane number** is the percentage of iso-octane in an iso-octane/n-heptane blend matching a fuel's knock. **Cetane number** measures diesel ignition quality against cetane and alpha-methylnaphthalene.

## Module 5 — Emissions, standards and electric vehicles

### Must know

- Pollutants: carbon monoxide, hydrocarbons, nitrogen oxides, smoke and particulates, sulphur oxides and lead.
- Photochemical smog from hydrocarbons and nitrogen oxides in sunlight.
- Crankcase control by PCV valve or fixed orifice; evaporative control by charcoal canister and purging.
- EGR, air injection and air aspirator; catalytic converter.
- Euro and Bharat Stage norms; Motor Vehicle Act.
- Electric vehicle parts and battery types.

### Definitions to reproduce

**EGR** recirculates a metered part of exhaust gas, 6 to 13 per cent, to lower peak combustion temperature and NOx. A **catalytic converter** uses platinum and palladium to oxidise HC and CO, and rhodium to reduce NOx. A **three-way catalyst** controls all three near stoichiometric mixture and needs unleaded fuel.

### Worked pattern

For EGR versus catalytic converter, compare what each controls, where it acts, and its limit. EGR lowers NOx at source but removes no CO or HC. The converter treats exhaust after combustion but is poisoned by lead and needs mixture control.

## Last 24 hours

Revise valve timing, thermosiphon cooling, pressure lubrication, synchromesh gearbox, Hotchkiss drive, hydraulic brake, ABS layout, steering geometry, battery ignition, centrifugal supercharger, turbocharger, EGR and catalytic converter. Memorise the dry/wet liner, supercharger/turbocharger and EGR/converter comparisons.

2. Explain the working of (i) the thermosiphon method of water cooling and (ii) a wax or Dole thermostat.
3. With a sketch, describe the splash lubrication method in IC engines. (10 marks)
4. Explain the working of a Hotchkiss drive in the automobile transmission system.
5. Explain the purpose of the antilock braking system in automobiles.
6. Analyse the battery ignition system with a circuit diagram, highlighting its advantages.
7. Illustrate with a sketch the centrifugal type of supercharger. (10 marks)
8. Describe the functioning of a Solex carburettor with a sketch. (10 marks)
9. Explain the importance of octane and cetane numbers, and the fuel-air mixture requirements of an SI engine with a sketch.
10. With a sketch, explain exhaust gas recirculation and the catalytic converter.
11. Enumerate the Bharat Stage VI norms for controlling automobile pollution and highlight the action planned to combat climate change in India.
