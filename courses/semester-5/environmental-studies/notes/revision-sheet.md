# Revision sheet — Environmental Studies

The banks cover five modules: biodiversity and ecosystems; pollution; waste; climate, water and mining; and environmental management. Most items are multiple choice, so revise definitions and the cause–effect–control chain for each topic.

## Module 1 — Biodiversity and ecosystems

### Must know

- Biodiversity has three levels: genetic, species and ecosystem diversity.
- A hotspot is rich in endemic species and under threat. India's include the Himalayas, the Western Ghats, Indo-Burma and Sundaland.
- Values are direct (food, medicine, timber), indirect (pollination, climate regulation) and ethical or aesthetic.
- Threats: habitat loss, invasive species, pollution, overexploitation and climate change. Habitat destruction is the primary cause.
- Conservation is in situ (national parks, sanctuaries, sacred groves) or ex situ (zoos, seed banks, cryopreservation).
- Ecosystem structure: producers, consumers and decomposers. Energy flow is unidirectional; nutrients are recycled; the energy pyramid is always upright.

### Definitions to reproduce

**Ecosystem** — a community of organisms interacting with one another and with their physical surroundings.

**Endemic species** — a species found only in one particular region.

## Module 2 — Pollution

### Must know

- Air pollutants are primary (SO₂, CO, particulates) or secondary (ozone, photochemical smog).
- Photochemical smog is driven by automobile emissions and sunlight. Acid rain forms when SO₂ and NOx produce sulphuric and nitric acid.
- Ozone depletion is caused by chlorofluorocarbons and halons; the Montreal Protocol addresses it.
- Water pollution: sewage and fertiliser runoff raise BOD and cause eutrophication. Nitrates and phosphates are the main nutrients.
- Noise limits are lower at night and in residential areas. Soil pollution comes from pesticides, waste and industrial spills.
- Key laws: Water Act 1974, Air Act 1981 and Environment (Protection) Act 1986.

### Definitions to reproduce

**Pollution** — the addition of a substance or form of energy that harms the environment or health.

**Eutrophication** — nutrient enrichment of water, leading to algal blooms and oxygen depletion.

### Worked pattern

Trace eutrophication in four steps: excess nitrate and phosphate enter a lake → algae bloom → bacteria decompose the dead algae → dissolved oxygen falls and fish die. Add controls: treat sewage, reduce fertiliser runoff and monitor BOD.

## Module 3 — Waste management

### Must know

- Categories: municipal solid waste, biomedical waste, hazardous waste and e-waste, each with separate handling rules.
- Biomedical waste includes sharps, infectious and pathological waste. It is segregated by colour-coded containers and treated by incineration, autoclaving or disinfection.
- Hazardous waste needs manifest tracking, secure storage and approved treatment.
- E-waste contains metals, plastics, lead and mercury; formal recycling recovers materials and avoids unsafe informal dismantling.
- Solid waste hierarchy: reduce, reuse, recycle, recover energy, then landfill. The first step in municipal solid waste management is collection.
- Landfill leachate must be collected and treated to protect groundwater.

### Definitions to reproduce

**Biomedical waste** — waste from diagnosis, treatment or immunisation, including sharps and infected materials.

**Hazardous waste** — waste that poses a substantial risk to health or the environment because of toxicity, reactivity, corrosivity or flammability.

## Module 4 — Climate change, water and mining

### Must know

- The primary cause of current climate change is greenhouse gas emissions from human activities, especially fossil fuels and deforestation.
- Greenhouse gases include carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide and fluorinated gases. Deforestation releases stored carbon and removes a sink.
- Carbon footprint is total emissions per person, organisation or product. Carbon trading uses permits or credits to price emissions.
- Groundwater depletion is a fall in levels due to over-extraction, mainly irrigation; effects include land subsidence and soil salinisation.
- Mining causes land degradation, acid mine drainage, dust and water pollution; control needs reclamation and treatment.

### Definitions to reproduce

**Carbon footprint** — total greenhouse gas emissions attributable to an activity, person or product.

**Groundwater depletion** — a decrease in groundwater levels due to overuse.

## Module 5 — Environmental management

### Must know

- Environmental Impact Assessment predicts and evaluates likely impacts before approval. Its first step is screening.
- Typical sequence: screening, scoping, baseline study, impact prediction, mitigation, management plan, public consultation, review, decision and monitoring.
- An Environmental Management System is a structured framework for monitoring and improving performance; ISO 14001 is the common standard.
- The Sustainable Development Goals are 17 goals under the UN Agenda 2030, covering poverty, health, energy, water, climate and life on land and below water.

### Definitions to reproduce

**EIA** — a formal process for predicting and evaluating the environmental impacts of a proposed project before approval.

**Sustainable development** — development that meets present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet theirs.

## Question bank

**Module 1**

1. What does biodiversity refer to? — Variety of genes, species and ecosystems.
2. Which biome has the highest biodiversity? — Tropical rainforest.
3. What is the primary cause of biodiversity loss? — Habitat destruction.
4. What is a keystone species? — A species with a disproportionate effect on its ecosystem.

**Module 2**

5. Which of the following is a secondary air pollutant? — Ozone.
6. The major source of photochemical smog in cities is — automobiles.
7. Which one of the following cause ozone layer depletion? — Methyl chloroform.
8. Which pollutant is primarily responsible for causing eutrophication in water bodies? — Nitrates and phosphates.

**Module 3**

9. What is the primary objective of biomedical waste management? — Ensure public health and safety.
10. What is considered hazardous waste? — Waste that poses a risk to health or the environment.
11. What is the first step in solid waste management? — Collection.

**Module 4**

12. What is the primary cause of global climate change? — Greenhouse gas emissions from human activities.
13. What is one way deforestation contributes to climate change? — It releases stored carbon dioxide.
14. What is groundwater depletion? — A decrease in groundwater levels due to overuse.

**Module 5**

15. What is the primary purpose of an Environmental Impact Assessment? — To predict and evaluate potential environmental impacts.
16. What is the first step in the EIA process? — Screening.
17. What is the primary goal of an Environmental Management System? — To monitor and improve environmental performance.
