# Revision sheet — Research Methodology and IPR

The syllabus has five modules: research methods in the first three and intellectual property in the final two. Papers ask 10-mark questions, so each answer needs a definition, a point list and one example.

## Exam pattern

CIE 50 and SEE 50. The SEE paper is set for 100 marks and scaled to 50: ten full questions of 20 marks, two from each module. Internally, two unit tests of 40 marks are scaled to 25, and the two CCAs are scaled to 25.

## Module 1 — Research methodology and the research problem

### Must know

- Research is a systematic, scientific search for knowledge. Its objectives are exploration, description, explanation, prediction and control.
- The process: formulate the problem, survey literature, develop a hypothesis, prepare the design, determine the sample, collect data, execute, analyse, test, generalise and report.
- Research methods are the techniques used; research methodology is the systematic study of those methods.
- A research problem is a definite gap or difficulty. Defining it fixes the concepts, variables and objectives.

### Definitions to reproduce

**Research** — a careful, systematic investigation to discover, interpret or revise facts.

### Worked pattern

For a question on selecting a research problem, use five criteria: subject knowledge, feasibility, relevance, availability of data and scope. Apply them to one example, such as dropout rates in a college, and state why the problem is researchable.

## Module 2 — Research design, sampling and data collection

### Must know

- Research design is the plan for collecting, measuring and analysing data. A good design is flexible, efficient and free from bias.
- Exploratory design suits a new problem; descriptive design describes what exists; diagnostic design identifies causes and effects.
- Experimental design rests on control, randomisation and replication.
- Sample design covers the objective, size, parameter of interest and selection method. Probability sampling gives every unit a known chance; non-probability sampling does not.
- Sampling error arises from studying a sample; non-sampling error arises from faulty collection, measurement, processing or non-response.

### Definitions to reproduce

**Research design** — the overall plan for obtaining answers to the research questions.

**Sampling error** — the difference between a sample estimate and the true population value because only part of the population is studied.

### Worked pattern

For selecting a data-collection method, use five factors: nature and scope of the enquiry, funds, time, precision required and investigator skills. Apply them to a consumer survey and state why a questionnaire or interview wins.

## Module 3 — Interpretation and report writing

### Must know

- Interpretation explains the meaning of findings and their relation to the objectives.
- Techniques: adequate analysis, attention to extreme values, pattern finding and consistency checks.
- Precautions: avoid over-generalisation, bias, false causation and ignoring contrary evidence.
- A report layout: title page, acknowledgement, contents, introduction, review, methodology, results, discussion, conclusion, references and appendices.
- Steps: logical analysis, outline, drafting, revision, formatting and presentation. Types: technical and popular.

### Definitions to reproduce

**Interpretation** — drawing inferences from analysed data and relating them to the research problem.

## Module 4 — Intellectual property, patents and copyright

### Must know

- Forms of IP: patents, copyright, trademarks, industrial designs, geographical indications, trade secrets and layout designs.
- TRIPS, administered by the WTO, sets minimum standards for IP protection and enforcement.
- A patent protects an invention. Conditions: novelty, inventive step, industrial applicability and patentable subject matter.
- A patent lasts 20 years from filing. The patentee can exclude others from making, using or selling the invention.
- Copyright protects literary, musical, dramatic and artistic works, films and sound recordings. It arises on creation, not registration.
- Copyright lasts for the author's life plus 60 years. The IT Act 2000 and Copyright Act 1957 govern internet issues.

### Definitions to reproduce

**Patent** — an exclusive right granted for an invention that is new, non-obvious and industrially useful.

**Copyright** — the exclusive right to reproduce, publish and adapt an original work.

### Worked pattern

For an IP comparison, use five rows: patent — invention — 20 years from filing; copyright — original work — life plus 60; trademark — brand identifier — 10 years, renewable; industrial design — aesthetic shape — 10 plus 5; IC layout — semiconductor layout — 10 years. Add one example to each.

## Module 5 — Trademarks, industrial designs and IC layout

### Must know

- A trademark is a visual symbol, word, name, device, label or colour combination that distinguishes goods or services.
- Registration requires graphical representation and distinctiveness. A registered trademark lasts 10 years and can be renewed indefinitely.
- Collective marks belong to an association and are used by its members. Certification marks certify origin, material or quality.
- Industrial design protects the aesthetic shape, configuration, pattern or ornament of an article. It must be new and original.
- IC layout design protects the original layout of transistors and connections. Registration lasts 10 years from filing or first commercial use.

### Definitions to reproduce

**Trademark** — a sign that distinguishes the goods or services of one undertaking from those of others.

**Industrial design** — the aesthetic features of shape, configuration, pattern or ornament applied to an article.

## Last 24 hours

- Write the research process and the five factors in method selection.
- Learn the difference between sampling and non-sampling error.
- Memorise the patent conditions, copyright term and trademark term.
- Rehearse the IP comparison table.
