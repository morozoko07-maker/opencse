# Revision sheet — Sustainable Development Goals

This sheet follows the five modules in the 2024-25 syllabus and three question banks in `material/SDG`. Syllabus code BCV605D, banks BHS605A; check your handout.

## Exam pattern

The syllabus gives CIE 50 + SEE 50, 40 hours and a three-hour SEE. The banks print no marks per question, so none are claimed below.

## Module 1 — Foundations of sustainable development

### Must know

- The Brundtland definition: development that meets present needs without compromising future generations.
- Three pillars: environmental, economic and social. The sustainability triangle balances them.
- The 17 goals: No Poverty; Zero Hunger; Good Health and Well-being; Quality Education; Gender Equality; Clean Water and Sanitation; Affordable and Clean Energy; Decent Work and Economic Growth; Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure; Reduced Inequalities; Sustainable Cities and Communities; Responsible Consumption and Production; Climate Action; Life Below Water; Life on Land; Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions; Partnerships for the Goals.
- Systems thinking studies interconnections, feedback loops and long-term effects. Linear thinking treats problems as separate steps.
- Life-cycle thinking covers extraction, production, use and disposal. Life-cycle assessment quantifies impacts at each stage.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Sustainable development meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.

> Systems thinking is a holistic approach that studies how parts of a system interact and affect one another.

## Module 2 — Engineering for environmental sustainability

### Must know

- Climate change is a long-term shift in temperature and weather patterns, driven mainly by greenhouse gases from fossil fuels, deforestation and industry.
- Renewable energy comes from sources replenished on a human timescale: solar, wind, hydro, biomass and geothermal.
- Green infrastructure uses natural and semi-natural systems: green roofs, parks, rain gardens, permeable pavements and restored wetlands.
- The waste hierarchy runs prevent, reuse, recycle, recover and dispose.
- A circular economy keeps materials in use through reuse, repair, remanufacture and recycling.
- Recycling makes new products of similar or lower quality; upcycling makes products of higher value.
- An EIA predicts environmental effects, proposes mitigation and checks compliance. Carbon footprint analysis totals direct and indirect emissions.

### Definitions to reproduce

> A circular economy minimises waste by keeping resources in use for as long as possible.

> An Environmental Impact Assessment evaluates the likely environmental effects of a proposed project before approval.

## Module 3 — Sustainable design, materials and technologies

### Must know

- Eco-friendly materials have low impact across their life cycle: bamboo, recycled metals and plastics, hempcrete, recycled glass, cork, rammed earth and low-VOC finishes.
- Sustainable manufacturing reduces energy, emissions and waste, and considers the full product life cycle.
- Smart cities use IoT, data analytics and digital services to manage energy, transport, water and waste.
- Green buildings combine energy efficiency, water efficiency, sustainable materials, indoor air quality and waste reduction.
- AI and IoT support monitoring, predictive maintenance, demand forecasting and leak detection.
- Smart grids balance supply and demand, integrate renewables and improve resilience.
- Biodegradable materials break down naturally but need suitable conditions; conventional plastics persist.

### Definitions to reproduce

> A smart city uses digital technology and data to improve infrastructure, services and quality of life while reducing environmental impact.

> Sustainable manufacturing creates products with minimal environmental impact and efficient use of resources.

## Module 4 — Socio-economic aspects and ethical responsibility

### Must know

- SDG 1 is No Poverty, SDG 3 is Good Health and Well-being, and SDG 6 is Clean Water and Sanitation.
- Engineering contributes through safe water systems, sanitation, affordable housing, health infrastructure and clean cooking.
- CSR is the company's responsibility for social and environmental effects beyond profit.
- Community-based solutions involve users in design and decisions. Stakeholder engagement identifies who gains and who bears the cost.
- Interdisciplinary work joins engineers with public health experts, economists, ecologists and local communities.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Corporate social responsibility is a company's obligation to consider social and environmental impacts alongside profit.

> Stakeholder engagement is the process of involving affected people and groups in decisions about a project.

## Module 5 — Innovation, case studies and future trends

### Must know

- Barriers are financial, political, social, technical and institutional. They delay projects and can shift costs to communities.
- Emerging trends: renewable energy, smart grids, energy storage, green hydrogen, carbon-neutral cities and circular supply chains.
- The post-2030 agenda asks what follows the SDGs: stronger monitoring, climate action and equity.

### Definitions to reproduce

> The post-2030 sustainability agenda is the set of priorities and targets that will follow the 2030 SDG deadline.

> A carbon-neutral city balances the greenhouse gases it emits with removal or offset, preferably through real reductions first.

## Last 24 hours

1. Write the Brundtland definition and the three pillars.
2. List the 17 SDGs in order.
3. Explain systems thinking versus linear thinking.
4. Link one engineering project to three SDGs and one trade-off.
5. List five barriers to sustainable engineering.

## Question bank

**Module 1**

1. Define sustainable development. Discuss briefly the 17 UN Sustainable Development Goals.
2. Describe the relevance of the SDGs to the field of engineering.
3. Apply systems thinking to assess the environmental impact of a product over its life cycle.
4. Differentiate between linear and systems thinking in engineering.

**Module 2**

5. Define climate change and renewable energy.
6. Define circular economy and explain how recycling differs from upcycling.
7. What is an Environmental Impact Assessment? Explain the process of carbon footprint analysis.

**Module 3**

8. List eco-friendly materials and explain how they can be used in manufacturing to meet sustainability.
9. Describe the integrated smart grid systems that enhance energy efficiency in smart cities.

**Module 4**

10. To meet SDG 1, 3 and 6, discuss the approaches to clean water, health and poverty.
11. Explain how interdisciplinary collaboration between engineers, public health experts and economists drives sustainable development outcomes.
12. Analyse the policy landscape for sustainable engineering in India.

**Module 5**

13. Describe the key barriers to implementing sustainable engineering projects and their impact on the environment.
14. Define the post-2030 sustainability agenda and describe the engineer's role in it.
15. List emerging renewable energy technologies and explain how smart grids support climate resilience.
