# Revision sheet — Yoga

This sheet covers the VI Semester content in the Yoga AEC syllabus (BNSK 659) and the theory question sheet. The syllabus is common to semesters III to VI, so use only the VI Semester asanas, pranayamas and last three limbs.

## Exam pattern

The syllabus gives CIE 100, SEE "-" and 26 contact hours. Mandatory, non-credit. Assessment is by two internal tests of 25 marks plus a final test of 50 marks, within CIE. No marks are printed on the questions.

## Ashtanga Yoga — Dharana, Dhyana and Samadhi

### Must know

- Patanjali's eight limbs: Yama, Niyama, Asana, Pranayama, Pratyahara, Dharana, Dhyana and Samadhi.
- Yama: ahimsa, satya, asteya, brahmacharya and aparigraha.
- Niyama: shaucha, santosha, tapas, svadhyaya and Ishvara pranidhana.
- Pratyahara withdraws the senses from outer objects. Dharana fixes attention on one object.
- Dharana is sustained concentration; Dhyana is meditative absorption in the same object; Samadhi is complete absorption, where the sense of subject and object dissolves.
- The three are stages of one inward movement. Dharana becomes steady as Dhyana, and Dhyana deepens into Samadhi.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Yoga is the suppression of the modifications of the mind.

> Dharana is focused concentration on a single object.

> Dhyana is meditative absorption in the object of concentration.

> Samadhi is the state of complete absorption in which the self and the object are no longer separated.

## Asanas

### Must know

- Asana is a steady and comfortable posture. The syllabus groups postures as sitting, standing, balancing, supine and prone.
- Ardha Padmasana: half-lotus. Place one foot on the opposite thigh and the other under the opposite leg; keep the spine erect. Avoid forcing the knees or hips.
- Yogamudra in Vajrasana: sit on the heels, take the hands behind the back, exhale and fold forward. Avoid with knee, back or neck injury, pregnancy or uncontrolled blood pressure.
- Vrikshasana: tree pose. Balance on one leg, place the other foot on the inner thigh or calf, and raise the hands. Avoid if the knee or ankle is injured.
- Garudasana: eagle pose. Bend the knees, wrap one leg around the other, entwine the arms and hold. Avoid with knee or shoulder injury.
- Veerabhadrasana 2: wide stance, arms extended, front knee over the ankle, head turned to the front hand.
- Veerabhadrasana 3: balance on one leg with the torso and rear leg horizontal. Avoid with dizziness or back injury.
- Sarvangasana: shoulder stand. Support the back, raise the legs and keep weight off the neck. Avoid with neck injury, high blood pressure or pregnancy.
- Setubandhasana: bridge. Lie on the back, bend the knees, lift the hips and keep the neck supported.
- Shavasana: lie flat, arms slightly away from the body, breathe naturally and relax.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Asana is a steady and comfortable physical posture.

> Shavasana is a supine relaxation posture in which the body is still and the breath is natural.

### Worked pattern

For an asana answer, give the starting position, the main movement, the final position, two precautions and three benefits. The examiner can see the technique, safety and effect without a long description.

## Kapalabhati

### Must know

- Kapalabhati is a cleansing breathing practice: forceful exhalation through both nostrils, passive inhalation, and a relaxed abdomen.
- The VI Semester practice is 80 strokes per minute for three rounds.
- It strengthens the abdominal muscles, improves lung ventilation, massages the digestive organs and increases alertness.
- Avoid with high blood pressure, hernia, pregnancy, recent abdominal surgery, nosebleeds or severe heart disease.
- Stop if dizziness, chest pain or breathlessness occurs.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Kapalabhati is a cleansing pranayama of forceful exhalations and passive inhalations.

## Pranayama

### Must know

- Pranayama is the regulation of breath. The syllabus for this semester covers Bhastrika and Bhramari.
- Bhastrika is the bellows breath: forceful inhalation and exhalation through both nostrils, using the abdomen. It increases energy and ventilation. Avoid with high blood pressure, heart disease, hernia or pregnancy.
- Bhramari is the humming-bee breath: close the ears, inhale, then exhale with a low humming sound. It calms the mind, reduces anxiety and helps sleep. Avoid with ear infection.
- In all pranayama, sit with an erect spine, breathe through the nose and stop at the first sign of strain.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Pranayama is the control or regulation of the breath.

> Bhramari is a pranayama in which exhalation is made with a humming sound.

## Last 24 hours

1. Write the eight limbs and the five Yamas and Niyamas.
2. Define Dharana, Dhyana and Samadhi in one sentence each.
3. Write one technique, two precautions and three benefits for Sarvangasana.
4. Write the technique and precautions for Kapalabhati at 80 strokes per minute.
5. Compare Bhastrika and Bhramari.
