# Revision sheet — Indian Knowledge System

This sheet follows the BIKS610 syllabus (2024-25) and the IA material in `material/IKS`. The course has three modules and no SEE; the IA-1 answer script is a scan, so the questions come from the typed descriptive sheet and the visible IA pages.

## Exam pattern

The syllabus gives CIE 100, SEE marks "-", 100 total marks and 13 lecture hours. It is non-credit and mandatory. No marks split is printed for individual questions. The MCQ and descriptive sheets supply the question formats.

## Module 1 — Introduction to Indian Knowledge Systems

### Must know

- IKS covers knowledge produced and transmitted in the Indian subcontinent: the Vedic corpus, philosophy, sciences, arts, crafts, medicine, agriculture and governance.
- The Vedic corpus has four Vedas: Rig, Yajur, Sama and Atharva. Each Veda has Samhita, Brahmana, Aranyaka and Upanishad layers.
- The six Vedangas are Shiksha (phonetics), Vyakarana (grammar), Nirukta (etymology), Chandas (metre), Jyotisha (astronomy and timekeeping) and Kalpa (ritual procedure).
- The six astika darshanas are Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga, Mimamsa and Vedanta. Buddhist, Jain and Charvaka systems are nastika.
- The syllabus names Ashtadasha Vidya, the eighteen fields of knowledge, as a way to describe the breadth of the corpus.
- Oral tradition preserved texts through recitation, memorisation and teaching lineages. It was accessible and adaptable but vulnerable to loss.
- Tacit knowledge is held in practice and experience; explicit knowledge is codified in text, formula or procedure. Written records travel better; tacit knowledge often stays with a community.
- Traditional knowledge is community-based and intergenerational. Indigenous knowledge is tied to a particular people and place. Western knowledge is not a single block, so compare methods rather than rank cultures.
- The descriptive sheet asks about decline during foreign invasions, deindustrialisation under British rule and international patents on turmeric, neem and basmati.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Indian Knowledge System is the body of knowledge, practice and belief developed in the Indian subcontinent and transmitted across generations.

> A Vedanga is a limb of the Veda: one of the six auxiliary disciplines needed to recite, understand and apply Vedic literature.

> Tacit knowledge is knowledge that is difficult to codify and is acquired through practice and experience.

## Module 2 — Traditional knowledge in humanities and sciences

### Must know

- Linguistics: Panini's Ashtadhyayi is a generative grammar of Sanskrit, built from rules, meta-rules and phonological analysis.
- Number and measurement: the decimal place-value system, zero as a number, negative numbers and large-number nomenclature are central contributions. Aryabhata, Brahmagupta and Bhaskara II are the names to attach to mathematics and astronomy.
- Mathematics: arithmetic, algebra, geometry, trigonometry and indeterminate equations appear in Indian texts. The syllabus also asks about standardised weights and measures.
- Chemistry: rasa shastra covers metallurgy, alchemy, dyes, perfumes and medicinal preparations.
- Physics: atomic theory, motion and sound are discussed in Vaisheshika and other schools.
- Astronomy and astrology: Jyotisha covers calendrical and observational astronomy; distinguish the two when writing an answer.
- Art: Natyashastra covers drama, dance, music and aesthetics, including rasa theory.
- Crafts and trade: guilds, textiles, metalwork, shipbuilding and inland and maritime trade routes supported specialised production.
- Engineering and technology: town planning, water management, metallurgy, architecture and mechanical devices are the main examples.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Ashtadhyayi is Panini's grammar of Sanskrit, organised as a system of rules for deriving words and sentences.

> Rasa shastra is the Indian tradition of chemistry and alchemy, including metallurgy and medicinal preparation.

## Module 3 — Traditional knowledge in professional domains

### Must know

- Town planning and architecture: Harappan cities show grid streets, drainage, wells and standardised bricks. Vastu Shastra texts discuss site selection, orientation and building layout.
- Construction: materials, masonry, water structures and temple architecture are the standard examples.
- Health, wellness and psychology: Ayurveda uses the tridosha framework, diagnosis, diet, lifestyle and herbal medicine. Yoga supplies physical, respiratory and meditative practices.
- Medicine: Ayurveda, Siddha and Unani are named systems in the wider tradition. Keep the answer specific to the system you discuss.
- Agriculture: crop rotation, soil classification, irrigation, seed selection and storage appear in texts such as Krishi Parashara and Vrikshayurveda.
- Governance and public administration: Kautilya's Arthashastra covers statecraft, taxation, law, espionage, welfare and economic policy.
- The syllabus links these domains to the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals: health, clean water, sustainable cities, responsible production and climate action.
- A good answer names the domain, gives one text or practice, explains the method and states its present relevance.

### Definitions to reproduce

> Ayurveda is a system of medicine that treats health as a balance of the body, senses, mind and spirit.

> The Arthashastra is Kautilya's treatise on statecraft, economic policy and military strategy.

## Last 24 hours

1. List the four Vedas, the six Vedangas and the six astika darshanas.
2. Explain tacit versus explicit knowledge with one example each.
3. Write five causes for the decline of the Indian knowledge tradition.
4. Name three patented traditional practices and the country or office involved.
5. Match five IKS domains to specific SDGs.

## Question bank

**Module 1**

1. Why is ancient knowledge, specifically within the Indian context, important for modern society?
2. Describe the concept of "Ashtadasha Vidya" (Eighteen Fields of Knowledge) in Indian literature.
3. What does the "System" aspect of IKS entail, and why is it important?
4. What advantages does explicit knowledge offer in terms of analysis and dissemination compared with tacit knowledge?
5. Discuss the importance of oral tradition in preserving Indian culture and knowledge, with at least two examples.
6. Explain the role of any two Vedangas in understanding Vedic literature.
7. Describe the key difference between tacit and explicit knowledge within the context of IKS.
8. Analyse the causes for the decline of the Indian knowledge tradition during foreign invasions.
9. Analyse the challenges imposed by foreign invasions on India's educational and cultural heritage.
10. Explain some examples of ancient practices, such as Ayurveda, that have been patented internationally.
11. Examine the process of deindustrialisation in India during British rule and the strategies used to control Indian industries and trade.
12. Explain the concept of the Pancha Mahabhuta temples, how three of them are geographically aligned, and the significance of that alignment.
